Assistant Research Professor, UCLA, IGPP, Los Angeles, California (1 July 2006 - Present)

UCLA, IGPP, Los Angeles, California (May 2004- July 2006)

Postdoctoral Researcher: currently I am working at UCLA and JPL/NASA coordinated research project, a project designed to answer the long standing problem; i.e, the plasmasphere contribution towards the Global Positioning System (GPS) signal degradation. We are imaging, tomographically, the top-side ionosphere and plasmasphere using data from the GPS receivers onboard (on Low Earth Orbiting (LEO) satellites). The application of my tomographic technique (see below) to the data obtained from GPS receivers onboard, has allowed the topside ionospheric and plasmaspheric electron density distribution to be determined. This is a relatively new research technique. Recently obtained results are very encouraging, demonstrating that the technique can make a significant contribution towards the efforts of improving the GPS navigation accuracy.

Postdoctoral Advisor:Prof Mark Moldwin

La Trobe University, Melbourne, Australia (September 2000 – May 2004).

Graduate research assistant: I pursued research to study the behavior of the ionosphere and plasmasphere using measurements of total electron content (TEC) obtained via GPS and other satellites such as TOPEX. Specifically, I implemented a tomographic imaging technique that permit us to determine details of ionospheric and plasmaspheric structure from the total electron content measurements. I developed my own tomography software from first principles (i.e., a direct implementation of the mathematics, as opposed to a rewrite of someone else’s code) using the Interactive Data Language (IDL) and FORTRAN. My tomographic software has been routinely validated using numerous results obtained by other ground- and space-based instruments. I also applied the technique to study the response of the ionosphere to magnetic storms and atmospheric gravity waves over the Australian region, which extends from equatorial regions to high latitudes, i.e. regions that have distinctly different behavior. Last certainly not least, my tomographic inversion technique can easily be applied to the study of body tissue calcification purpose, if necessary.
